A mug does not wear out, it gets lost

Mugs are the rare category where the material never wears. Ceramic does not fatigue, stainless does not rust through, and the glaze outlives the cabinet it sits in. The ledger still shows mug spending, because the deaths are mechanical and logistical: the rim chips on a sink edge, the handle snaps in a dishwasher rack, or the mug walks out of an office under someone else's coffee. How a mug dies decides what it costs, and none of those deaths appear on the price tag.

The loss ledger is the expensive one. A $12 ceramic mug that leaves the household once every two years, chipped, cracked, or abandoned on a desk, runs $6 a year, which is $120 across twenty years. The mug itself never wore out; the replacement cadence is the entire bill. Raising the sticker raises that bill in proportion, because a fragile mug and a tough one are equally easy to lose.

The failure ledger is the cheap one. A $34 insulated steel mug survives the deaths that end ceramic: the drop dents it, the sink edge scratches it, the rim passes the chip test. Kept 17 years and then lost, it costs $34 over 17 years, $2 a year; a twenty-year window with one replacement runs $68 / 20 = $3.40 a year. The gap is not materials, it is death count: the ceramic column logs ten funerals in twenty years, the steel column two.

Ranking mugs by how they die instead of how they pour is the Durable Picks method, and the full mug ledger lives at durablepicks.com.

The coffee goes cold in an hour either way. The bill runs twenty years.
